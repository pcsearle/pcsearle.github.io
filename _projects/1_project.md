Have you ever wondered how fish see in the deep-sea? As light attenuates through water, the available light spectrum rapidly diminishes and is eventually lost. Visual systems have evolved to capture light in this challenging environment at behavioral, morphological, physiological, and molecular levels. I am particularly interested in understanding how opsins-a universal component of visual systems in all light-sensitive organisms that detect and respond to environmental stimuli-respond to environmental pressures. 

Rockfish (<i>Sebastes</i>)&mdash;a diverse genus of marine fishes&mdash;are an excellent system to tease apart how changes in light availability mediate opsin evolution. Rockfish evolved in the Miocene ~9-8 MYA in the Northwest Pacific Ocean and underwent subsequent expansions into the Northeast Pacific Ocean, Gulf of California, and Atlantic Ocean. Further, rockfish inhabit an impressive range of habitats from 0 m to 1,500 m in depth. By comparing opsin genes across species that have independently colonized such different light environments, I will provide new insight on the molecular basis of visual adaptation to depth.
