I study how animals evolve, develop, and are shaped by their environments, with a focus on freshwater and marine fishes. My work spans visual ecology, phylogenetics, developmental biology, and conservation &mdash; usually starting from a simple question about why an organism looks or behaves the way it does, and working backward to the evolutionary or ecological process responsible.

        <h3>Vision Across Depth</h3>
        <p>Light changes dramatically with depth, and visual systems have to keep up. I study how opsins &mdash; the light-sensing proteins at the heart of vision &mdash; evolve as species move into new light environments, using rockfish as a natural experiment in vision across a huge depth gradient.</p>

        <h3>Resolving Species Boundaries</h3>
        <p>Where does one species end and another begin? I use complete mitochondrial genomes and other genomic data to untangle the relationships between closely related, often hard-to-distinguish lineages &mdash; from cutthroat trout and suckers to rockfish and lichen-forming fungi &mdash; work that also informs real conservation and management decisions.</p>
    </div>
</div>

        <h3>Shape Change Through Development</h3>
        <p>An animal's body shape isn't fixed &mdash; it's a moving target across its lifetime. I study how developmental timing shapes morphology as animals grow, and how shifts in that timing between closely related species can drive some of the physical differences we use to tell them apart.</p>
