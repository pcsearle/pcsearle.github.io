I study how animals evolve, develop, and are shaped by their environments, with a focus on freshwater and marine fishes. My work spans visual ecology, phylogenetics, and conservation genetics &mdash; usually starting from a simple question about why an organism looks or behaves the way it does, and working backward to the evolutionary or ecological process responsible.

        <h3>Visual Ecology</h3>
        <p>Light changes dramatically with depth, and visual systems have to keep up. I study how opsins &mdash; the light-sensing proteins at the heart of vision &mdash; evolve as species move into new light environments, using rockfish as a natural experiment in vision across a huge depth gradient.</p>

        <h3>Phylogenetics</h3>
        <p>Where does one species end and another begin? I use complete mitochondrial genomes and other genomic data to untangle the relationships between closely related, often hard-to-distinguish lineages &mdash; from cutthroat trout and suckers to rockfish and lichen-forming fungi.</p>
